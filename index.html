About MJC

Mahjong Coach (MJC) introduces beginning and intermediate Mahjong players to Six Box Theory™ (6BT) and helps them put it into practice to understand hand structure and make better playing decisions.

MJC reveals the structure hidden within a Mahjong hand.

Hand History

Welcome to Mahjong Coach

Learn. Understand. Improve.

The purpose of Mahjong Coach (MJC) is to introduce beginning and intermediate Mahjong players to Six Box Theory™ (6BT), a method for understanding Mahjong hand structure, and help them put it into practice to make better playing decisions.

Currently, MJC supports 16-Tile Mahjong styles such as Filipino and Taiwanese, with a version adapted for Chinese 13-Tile Mahjong coming soon.

Getting Started

Welcome to Mahjong Coach!

To get started:

  1. Select your Role: Player or Dealer.
  2. Select your Ruleset: Choose the Mahjong rules you play by. (Currently, MJC supports Filipino and Taiwanese 16-Tile Mahjong. Chinese 13-Tile adaptation coming soon.)
  3. Select your Display preferences: Choose the order in which your tiles are displayed.
  4. Enter your starting tiles: Tap each tile to enter the tiles in your hand.
  5. Review your settings and tiles: Make sure everything is correct.
  6. Press Play On! MJC will organize your hand according to Six Box Theory™.

About 6BT

Six Box Theory™ (6BT) provides a method for understanding Mahjong hand structure.

Rather than viewing a hand as individual tiles, 6BT organizes it into boxes, offering a simpler way to visualize, understand, and develop your hand.

This makes it easier to recognize strengths and weaknesses, improve tile efficiency, and make better discard decisions.

Understanding the Boxes

To help you better see the relationships between the tiles in your hand, Six Box Theory™ organizes your hand into boxes.

Complete Boxes (CB)
Completed melds that require no additional tiles. These appear at the bottom of the display.

Developing Boxes (DB)
Groups of tiles that are working toward becoming Complete Boxes. These tile groups are called Candidates. These appear in the middle of the display.

Reserves (R)
Tiles not currently assigned to a CB or DB. These appear at the top of the display.

Complete six boxes—five melds and one Eye— and That's Mahjong!

Understanding Pathways

Pathways show how a Developing Box (DB) can advance to a Complete Box.

Green ↓ — Same-Suit Tile
A different numbered tile of the same suit can advance the DB toward completion.

Orange ↓ — Matching Tile
Another copy of a tile already in the DB can advance it toward completion.

Acceptance
The number of tile opportunities that can advance a DB toward completion.

Sources
How a needed tile can enter your hand—through your own draw or a discard you can claim.

Effective Acceptance
The number above each arrow represents the remaining tile opportunities that can advance the Developing Box toward completion through that pathway.

Understanding Release Tile (RT)

Release Tile (RT) lets you free a tile from a Hidden Complete Box to complete another grouping. MJC shows you the resulting structure before anything changes—you decide whether to keep it.

Meet the Candidates

They're called Candidates because each holds the potential to become a Complete Box. As the hand develops, some Candidates will become Complete Boxes while others may change or be replaced.

Pong-Kang Candidate (PKC)
A four-tile Developing Box that can be declared as a Kang or NEWS.

Chow-Pong Candidate (CPC)
Three or four suited tiles that can develop into either a Chow or a Pong.

Eye-Pong Candidate (EPC)
Two matching tiles that can serve as the Eye or develop into a Pong.

Eye Candidate (EC)
Two matching tiles being held as the Eye.

Chow Candidates
Two suited tiles that are working toward becoming a Chow.

Double-Sided Wait (DSW)
Two consecutive suited tiles that can complete a Chow from either end.

Middle Wait (MW)
Two suited tiles with one number between them, waiting for the middle tile to complete the Chow.

Edge Wait (EW)
A 1–2 or 8–9 combination waiting for the single tile that completes the Chow.

Half-Eye (HE)
A single tile being held as a possible half of a pair for the Eye.

How to Fix Your Hand

Use Fix Hand when MJC doesn't match your hand.

Review your Existing Melds. Change a meld to Hidden, Exposed, or Remove it. Use Add Meld for a missing meld.

2. Enter Your Free Tiles.
Enter only tiles not committed to a meld. Do not enter your Melded Tiles again. MJC shows how many Free Tiles you need.

Understanding the Boxes

Six Box Theory™ is a method for understanding Mahjong hand structure. In Coaching View, MJC shows which groupings are complete, which are developing, and which tiles remain outside those groupings.

The examples below use tile names and numbers in place of tile images. Where a suit is named, every numbered tile in that example belongs to that suit.

1. The Six-Box Goal

For the usual 16-tile hand structure, the goal is five melds and one Eye: six Complete Boxes. The winning hand normally contains 17 tiles, with additional tiles for four-tile melds such as Kangs.

A box represents a structural grouping, not a fixed number of tiles. An Eye has two tiles, a Chow or Pong has three, and a Kang has four.

Special hands such as Siete Pares / Seven Pairs and Escalera / Straight use different structural targets. MJC adjusts the displayed box target when you pursue those hands.

A Complete Box (CB) contains a completed grouping. The main examples are:

5. Reserves

Reserves are tiles outside MJC’s displayed Developing and Complete Boxes. They may later join a grouping as new tiles enter your hand.

A Reserve and a Half Eye may each contain a single tile. Their placement tells you the difference: a Half Eye occupies a Developing Box as a possible Eye, while a Reserve sits outside the displayed boxes.

Example

A lone 5 Bam in Reserves might gain a partner when you draw 4 Bam or 6 Bam. MJC can then display the two tiles together as a developing grouping.

Being a Reserve does not automatically make a tile the best discard. Use the current recommendation and its Insight explanation to understand MJC’s assessment.

6. Reading Your Structure

Start with the Complete Boxes. Then look at the Developing Boxes and what each needs. Finally, examine the Reserves. This gives you a practical way to review your hand’s structure.

CB and DB headings include box numbers. Those numbers identify positions in the displayed structure; they are not tile quantities or strength ratings.

An Empty Developing Box marks an unfilled structural position. It contains no tiles.

The displayed arrangement can change as tiles enter or leave your hand. A candidate may complete, change type, or give way to another grouping. A tile belongs to only one displayed grouping at a time.

Use Labels: Full while learning the names. Once the abbreviations become familiar, shortened labels offer a more compact display.

Understanding Pathways

In this chapter

Pathways show how supported Developing Boxes can advance toward completion. They help you connect the tiles already grouped in a box with the incoming tiles that could help it.

1. Turn Pathways On

  1. Open your hand in Coaching View.
  2. Tap Pathways: On to show pathway information.
  3. Examine the markers and numbers added to supported Developing Boxes.

Once Pathways are displayed, the button reads Pathways: Off, offering to hide them. Pathways Explained opens a quick reference.

In Standard View, the Pathways control reads Pathways: NA and is unavailable. Switch to Coaching View to use it.

2. Read the Colored Markers

The marker color identifies the kind of incoming tile that advances the grouping.

Green ↓ — Same-Suit Tile
A different numbered tile of the same suit can advance the grouping.

Orange ↓ — Matching Tile
Another copy of a tile already in the grouping can advance it.

For example, adding 3 Bam to 4 Bam · 5 Bam follows a same-suit pathway. Adding Red to Red · Red follows a matching-tile pathway.

The colors identify pathway types. They do not mark a tile for discard.

3. Read the Numbers

Acceptance
The number of tile opportunities that can advance the Developing Box toward completion.

Sources
How a needed tile can enter your hand: through your own draw or through an eligible discard claim. Claim eligibility depends on the grouping and game rules.

Effective Acceptance (EA)
The number above a pathway marker. It represents the remaining tile opportunities MJC calculates for that particular pathway.

Count opportunities, not promises

These figures describe opportunities based on MJC’s recorded information. They do not guarantee that a tile will arrive, identify the next draw, or show a percentage chance of winning.

A tile type and a tile opportunity are different things. A DSW may need either of two tile types, with multiple copies of each type potentially contributing to its opportunities.

Matching-tile pathways also help explain an Eye-Pong Candidate becoming a Pong or a Half Eye developing into a pair. The grouping determines what that matching tile accomplishes.

6. Use Pathways in Your Decisions

  1. Identify the Developing Box and the tiles it contains.
  2. Read the marker colors to distinguish same-suit and matching-tile pathways.
  3. Identify the incoming tiles that advance each pathway.
  4. Read the EA figures and the Acceptance and Sources summaries.
  5. Consider how the grouping fits the rest of your hand.

More opportunities can make a grouping easier to develop, but a single figure does not settle every decision. Your Eye, completed melds, other candidates, and the current stage of play also matter.

Pathways explain development options. For MJC’s current discard suggestion, read Recommended Discard and Insight. Chapter 9 explains that coaching.

Understanding Release Tile (RT)

In this chapter
  • What Release Tile Does
  • When RT Is Available
  • Select a Tile and Grouping
  • Preview and Confirm
  • Back, Cancel, and No Alternatives

1. What Release Tile Does

Release Tile (RT) lets you explore a different arrangement by freeing a tile from an eligible hidden Complete Box and using it in another grouping.

The tile stays in your hand. RT changes its structural assignment; it does not draw, claim, or discard a tile.

Example: the same tiles, a different arrangement

Before

Hidden Chow:
1 Char · 2 Char · 3 Char
Developing grouping:
4 Char · 5 Char

After regrouping

Developing grouping:
1 Char · 2 Char
Hidden Chow:
3 Char · 4 Char · 5 Char

Teaching example; tile names replace images. Available RT choices depend on the full recorded hand.

In this example, moving the 3 Char completes one Chow and leaves 1 Char · 2 Char developing. You still hold exactly the same tiles.

An alternate arrangement is not automatically an improvement. RT lets you inspect the result before deciding whether to keep it.

2. When RT Is Available

Look for RT beside an eligible Complete Box heading in Current Hand Coaching View while preparing to draw or claim.

Eligible boxes are hidden melds. Exposed melds and the Eye do not offer this control. RT is not offered during the ordinary discard step.

The RT Explained link provides a short explanation when Release Tile controls are available.

3. Select a Tile and Grouping

  1. Tap RT beside the hidden Complete Box you want to explore.
  2. In Select Tile to Release, tap the tile you want to move out of that grouping.
  3. If viable alternatives are available, MJC displays Select Alternate Grouping.
  4. Review the listed Developing Boxes and tap the destination grouping.

Each alternate grouping shows its box number, grouping type, and tiles. Choose by those details so you direct the released tile to the intended grouping.

Only the alternatives MJC finds for the selected tile are listed. RT does not let you place a tile into any box arbitrarily.

4. Preview and Confirm

Selecting an alternate grouping opens Preview. The Expected Results area shows the affected groupings in the proposed arrangement.

Before confirming, check:
  • What remains of the original Complete Box?
  • What does the destination grouping become?
  • Does the resulting arrangement support your intended plan?

The preview focuses on affected groupings. It is not a complete redisplay of every unchanged box in your hand.

Tap Confirm to apply the previewed arrangement. MJC returns to the hand display with the updated structure.

Confirming RT does not consume a draw or discard. Continue with the action shown in the hand instruction.

5. Back, Cancel, and No Alternatives

Back
From Preview, returns to the alternate-grouping step. From that step, returns to tile selection. Use it to explore another choice before confirming.

Cancel
Closes the RT process without applying the proposed arrangement.

No viable alternate groupings available
This notice means MJC found no supported destination for the tile you selected. Tap OK to close the notice.

If you want to explore another tile, reopen RT and make a different selection. Finding no alternative for one tile does not mean every tile in that hidden meld has the same result.

Use RT to investigate structure deliberately: inspect the starting grouping, inspect the alternative, then confirm only when you want the displayed result.

Recommendations and Coaching

In this chapter
  • Read a Discard Recommendation
  • Open Insight
  • Understand the Reasoning
  • Handle Equal Choices
  • Read Meld Recommendations
  • Read Coaching Alerts

MJC’s coaching helps you understand the structure of your recorded hand and the reasons behind a suggested choice. You decide which action to take in your actual game.

1. Read a Discard Recommendation

In Coaching View, MJC can display a discard recommendation when your hand is ready for a discard. This includes the Dealer’s first discard from the Starting Hand Display.

Recommended Discard: White.
Hide Insight

Example recommendation; the suggested tile depends on your recorded hand.

Read the tile name carefully, including its suit where applicable. The recommendation does not discard the tile for you. Select the tile and complete the normal discard confirmation when you decide to use it.

Tap Hide if you want to consider your own choice first. Tap Show Recommended Discards to reveal the recommendation again.

Recommendations are not displayed at every step. They are unavailable in Standard View, during acquisition or replacement draws, and after the hand is complete. The current special-hand pursuits also suppress ordinary discard recommendations.

2. Open Insight

Tap Insight beside the hand-display recommendation to open Why This Discard?

The explanation identifies the recommended tile or choices and describes the structural effect of discarding one. Recommended tile names appear in bold.

Read for the effect

Does the discard remove a Reserve? Does it break a Developing Box? What remains afterward? Which groupings stay intact?

Insight explains the current recommendation. Revisit it after your hand changes rather than assuming an earlier explanation still applies.

The separate Insight control on the Discard Tile Screen currently displays a coming-soon message. Use the hand-display recommendation’s Insight for the working explanation.

3. Understand the Reasoning

When Reserves are available

MJC compares their contribution to developing the hand. Its explanations use Connectivity, then Centricity, to describe the comparison.

Connectivity concerns nearby same-suit support. Centricity concerns a suited tile’s position toward the middle of the suit. Refer to the Glossary for these terms.

Discarding a Reserve leaves the displayed Developing and Complete Boxes intact. That structural effect is part of the explanation.

When there are no Reserves

MJC must consider a tile from an existing developing grouping. Insight explains which grouping changes and what is preserved.

Depending on the structure, MJC may compare Chow Candidates’ completion opportunities, consider removing a tile from a Chow-Pong Candidate, or weigh preserving an Eye Candidate against other developing options.

Some comparisons also take account of the hand’s progress, using your recorded discards. A recommendation can therefore change as play advances even when familiar grouping types remain in the hand.

Read the current explanation for the actual comparison being made. A useful recommendation describes both what you give up and what you retain.

4. Handle Equal Choices

Sometimes MJC gives equal preference to more than one discard. It may list tile names joined by “or”, or summarize a larger set and direct you to Insight.

Possible forms

Recommended Discard: White or East.

Recommended Discard: Any of your 3 Reserves.

These are examples of alternative recommendations, not instructions to discard multiple tiles.

Discard only one tile. Equal preference means MJC’s current assessment does not distinguish those choices. It does not mean they will produce identical future hands.

Open Insight to see the named choices and the reason they are tied.

5. Read Meld Recommendations

When an incoming tile offers multiple meld groupings, MJC may mark a recommended choice. Compare the tiles in each grouping before selecting one.

If MJC assesses the choices as equally sound, it may say “MJC: Either choice is structurally sound.”

A meld recommendation and a discard recommendation address different decisions. Choosing a meld determines the grouping to record. Choosing a discard determines the tile to release afterward, when a discard is required.

Chapter 6 explains the meld-choice and declaration controls.

6. Read Coaching Alerts

Coaching View may display messages that call attention to the current structure or pursuit.

BOLO for Eyes

“BOLO” means “be on the lookout.” This alert draws attention to the need to develop an Eye.

Protect Your Only Eye

Draws attention to your only Eye Candidate. Consider the effect before using its tiles in another grouping.

Over-Paired

Draws attention to an excess of pairs in the current structure. Review how those pairs support your intended hand and whether a special-hand option is relevant.

Pursuing a Special Hand

Messages such as Pursuing Siete Pares or Pursuing Escalera identify the active pursuit. Names may differ with the ruleset, such as Seven Pairs or Straight.

Kang Deferred

Reminds you that a Kang declaration has been postponed. It does not mean the declaration or its replacement draw has already been completed.

Treat alerts as prompts to inspect your hand. They do not replace the current action instruction, declare a meld, or make a discard.

Accurate coaching depends on an accurate recorded hand. If MJC’s tiles or melds no longer match your actual hand, correct the record before relying on its next recommendation.

Completing a Hand

In this chapter
  • Recognize Mahjong Watch
  • Record the Winning Tile
  • Read the Completion Message

  • Begin Another Hand

Keep recording your actions until the winning tile completes your hand. How that tile entered your hand matters: record a draw from the wall differently from a claimed discard.

1. Recognize Mahjong Watch

Mahjong Watch identifies a hand waiting for a completing tile. For the usual six-box structure, five Complete Boxes leave the final grouping to complete.

When MJC recognizes Mahjong Watch during the acquisition step, the usual four acquisition controls are replaced by Draw and Mahjong.

Mahjong Watch
Draw
Mahjong

Choose according to where the incoming tile comes from.

Being on Mahjong Watch does not mean every incoming tile wins. Continue recording the actual tile. If it does not complete your hand, follow the next instruction and continue play.

2. Record the Winning Tile

Winning by Draw

  1. Draw your actual tile from the wall.
  2. Tap Draw.
  3. Select the number and suit, or the honor tile’s name.
  4. Tap the tile image to record it.

MJC evaluates the hand after the tile is recorded. If the tile completes the hand, MJC displays the completion message.

Winning on an Opponent’s Discard

  1. Identify the discarded tile you are claiming to complete your hand.
  2. Tap Mahjong.
  3. Select and record that tile through the tile-selection screen.

Use this winning-claim control when completing Mahjong on a discard, rather than recording the tile as a draw from the wall.

Example: waiting for White

You draw White from the wall: use Draw.

An opponent discards White and you claim it to win: use Mahjong.

3. Read the Completion Message

MJC displays “That’s Mahjong!” when it recognizes a completed hand. In Filipino mode, a win recorded from your own draw displays “Bunot!”

That’s Mahjong!

Bunot!
Filipino win by draw

Once the hand is complete, the ordinary acquisition and discard cycle stops. Do not record another discard from the winning hand.

Active special-hand pursuits use their own completion structure. Read the displayed target and resulting completion message rather than applying the ordinary six-box count to every special hand.

MJC’s completion message reflects the hand and ruleset recorded in the app. Keep that record aligned with your actual tiles and your table’s rules.

4. Review and Save

Review the final tiles and melds. Check that the winning tile and its source were recorded correctly.

If the most recent entry was wrong, use Correct Last Entry when available. Correct the record before treating the displayed result as your actual hand outcome.

Tap Save Hand if you want to keep the hand for later review. Open Hand History to locate the saved entry and view its transcript.

Keep a learning record

Saving is useful for more than wins. You can also save an unfinished recorded hand for review. Chapter 11 explains saved hands, transcripts, details, and sharing.

5. Begin Another Hand

Save anything you want to keep before selecting New Hand. Then follow the app’s prompts to begin the next hand.

On the Tile Selection Screen, check the new hand’s Role, Ruleset, and Display settings. The dealer may have changed, so do not assume the previous role still applies.

Enter the new starting tiles, verify their identities and quantities, then press Play On!
