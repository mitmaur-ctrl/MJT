About MJC

Mahjong Coach (MJC) introduces beginning and intermediate Mahjong players to Six Box Theory™ (6BT) and helps them put it into practice to understand hand structure and make better playing decisions.

MJC reveals the structure hidden within a Mahjong hand.

Hand History

Welcome to Mahjong Coach

Learn. Understand. Improve.

The purpose of Mahjong Coach (MJC) is to introduce beginning and intermediate Mahjong players to Six Box Theory™ (6BT), a method for understanding Mahjong hand structure, and help them put it into practice to make better playing decisions.

Currently, MJC supports 16-Tile Mahjong styles such as Filipino and Taiwanese, with a version adapted for Chinese 13-Tile Mahjong coming soon.

Getting Started

Welcome to Mahjong Coach!

To get started:

  1. Select your Role: Player or Dealer.
  2. Select your Ruleset: Choose the Mahjong rules you play by. (Currently, MJC supports Filipino and Taiwanese 16-Tile Mahjong. Chinese 13-Tile adaptation coming soon.)
  3. Select your Display preferences: Choose the order in which your tiles are displayed.
  4. Enter your starting tiles: Tap each tile to enter the tiles in your hand.
  5. Review your settings and tiles: Make sure everything is correct.
  6. Press Play On! MJC will organize your hand according to Six Box Theory™.

About 6BT

Six Box Theory™ (6BT) provides a method for understanding Mahjong hand structure.

Rather than viewing a hand as individual tiles, 6BT organizes it into boxes, offering a simpler way to visualize, understand, and develop your hand.

This makes it easier to recognize strengths and weaknesses, improve tile efficiency, and make better discard decisions.

Understanding the Boxes

To help you better see the relationships between the tiles in your hand, Six Box Theory™ organizes your hand into boxes.

Complete Boxes (CB)
Completed melds that require no additional tiles. These appear at the bottom of the display.

Developing Boxes (DB)
Groups of tiles that are working toward becoming Complete Boxes. These tile groups are called Candidates. These appear in the middle of the display.

Reserves (R)
Tiles not currently assigned to a CB or DB. These appear at the top of the display.

Complete six boxes—five melds and one Eye— and That's Mahjong!

Understanding Pathways

Pathways show how a Developing Box (DB) can advance to a Complete Box.

Green ↓ — Same-Suit Tile
A different numbered tile of the same suit can advance the DB toward completion.

Orange ↓ — Matching Tile
Another copy of a tile already in the DB can advance it toward completion.

Acceptance
The number of tile opportunities that can advance a DB toward completion.

Sources
How a needed tile can enter your hand—through your own draw or a discard you can claim.

Effective Acceptance
The number above each arrow represents the remaining tile opportunities that can advance the Developing Box toward completion through that pathway.

Understanding Release Tile (RT)

Release Tile (RT) lets you free a tile from a Hidden Complete Box to complete another grouping. MJC shows you the resulting structure before anything changes—you decide whether to keep it.

Meet the Candidates

They're called Candidates because each holds the potential to become a Complete Box. As the hand develops, some Candidates will become Complete Boxes while others may change or be replaced.

Pong-Kang Candidate (PKC)
A four-tile Developing Box that can be declared as a Kang or NEWS.

Chow-Pong Candidate (CPC)
Three or four suited tiles that can develop into either a Chow or a Pong.

Eye-Pong Candidate (EPC)
Two matching tiles that can serve as the Eye or develop into a Pong.

Eye Candidate (EC)
Two matching tiles being held as the Eye.

Chow Candidates
Two suited tiles that are working toward becoming a Chow.

Double-Sided Wait (DSW)
Two consecutive suited tiles that can complete a Chow from either end.

Middle Wait (MW)
Two suited tiles with one number between them, waiting for the middle tile to complete the Chow.

Edge Wait (EW)
A 1–2 or 8–9 combination waiting for the single tile that completes the Chow.

Half-Eye (HE)
A single tile being held as a possible half of a pair for the Eye.

How to Fix Your Hand

Use Fix Hand when MJC doesn't match your hand.

Review your Existing Melds. Change a meld to Hidden, Exposed, or Remove it. Use Add Meld for a missing meld.

2. Enter Your Free Tiles.
Enter only tiles not committed to a meld. Do not enter your Melded Tiles again. MJC shows how many Free Tiles you need.

Getting Your Hand Ready

In this chapter
  • Choose Your Role
  • Check Your Ruleset
  • Choose Your Display Order
  • Enter Your Starting Tiles
  • Review and Play On!

Begin on the Tile Selection Screen (TSS). Set up MJC to match the hand you are about to play, then enter the tiles you were dealt. Keep your actual tiles nearby so you can check your entry.

1. Choose Your Role

Tap Role. Select Player if you are not the dealer, or Dealer if you are dealing this hand. Tap Save.

Player
16
starting tiles
Dealer
17
starting tiles

Check that the target beside Tiles Entered matches your role.

2. Check Your Ruleset

Tap Ruleset and select Filipino 16-Tile or Taiwanese 16-Tile to match your game.

For Filipino Mahjong, choose Honors Enabled or Honors Disabled. Honors are the Winds and Dragons. With Honors enabled, also check whether N-E-W-S Allowed matches your table’s rules.

Tap Save when finished. Chinese 13-Tile is marked Feature Coming Soon.

3. Choose Your Display Order

Tap Display. Choose your First Suit and Second Suit. The remaining suit automatically becomes the Third Suit.

Under Honors Order, choose Winds before Dragons or Dragons before Winds. Tap Save.

Use an order you find easy to follow. This setting controls how suit and honor groups appear in MJC’s tile displays and selection screens.

4. Enter Your Starting Tiles

Work through your actual hand one suit or honor group at a time. Find each tile on the TSS and tap it once for each copy you hold.

For example, if you have two copies of 5 Char, tap its tile twice from a count of zero.

Example: two copies of 5 Char
5 Char
2

The tile name replaces its image in this illustration.

Check the quantity shown on each selected tile. A blank quantity means zero. Each tap cycles the count from zero through four, then back to zero. Long-press a tile to clear its count.

Watch Tiles Entered as you work. Continue until all your starting tiles are recorded: 16 for Player or 17 for Dealer.

If you need to start the entry again, tap Clear Tiles. Undo Clear appears temporarily if you need to restore the cleared tiles.

5. Review and Play On!

Before continuing, compare your entry with your actual hand:

  • Your Role and Ruleset are correct.
  • Each tile is in the correct suit or honor group.
  • The quantity of each tile matches your hand.
  • The entered total matches the required target.

Understanding Your Hand Display

In this chapter
  • Starting and Current Hand
  • Standard View
  • Coaching View
  • Counters and Display Options
  • Messages and Tile Highlights

MJC reveals the structure hidden within a Mahjong hand. Learning to read its displays helps you follow your hand as tiles are drawn, claimed, and discarded.

1. Starting and Current Hand

Starting Hand shows the tiles you entered before beginning play. Review them against your actual hand. Use Revise if you need to correct the entry.

Current Hand shows your recorded hand as play progresses. It updates as you record your actions. Keep those actions in step with your actual game so MJC continues to show the hand you hold.

Starting and Current describe when you are viewing the hand. Standard and Coaching describe how MJC presents it. Both hand displays offer both views.

2. Standard View

Starting Hand Standard View groups your tiles by suit and honors. The groups follow the order selected in Display.

Current Hand Standard View separates your tiles into three areas:

Loose Tiles
Tiles outside Complete Boxes, grouped by suit and honors.
Hidden Melds
Completed groupings recorded as hidden.
Exposed Melds
Completed groupings recorded as exposed.

Area overview; tile images are omitted.

“Loose” describes where MJC displays these tiles. They may still form pairs or other developing groupings.

3. Coaching View

Tap Coaching View to see your hand organized using Six Box Theory™. MJC presents its developing and completed structures, along with any Reserves.

Reserves
Tiles outside the displayed Developing and Complete Boxes.
Developing Boxes
Groupings that still need tiles to become complete.
Completed Boxes
Completed groupings, including melds and the Eye.

Area overview; tile images are omitted.

Box headings identify each grouping. DB means Developing Box; CB means Complete Box. Meld headings also identify whether the meld is Hidden or Exposed.

Switching views changes the presentation of your recorded hand. It does not draw, discard, or change any tiles. The view button names the view you can switch to: while Coaching View is shown, the button reads Standard View.

Chapter 4 explains the boxes and their grouping types in detail.

4. Counters and Display Options

Boxes Complete shows how many of the required boxes are complete. The usual six-box structure consists of five melds and an Eye. Special-hand pursuits use their own box targets.

Tiles In Hand counts tiles outside Complete Boxes. It is not the total number of tiles in your entire recorded hand. Tiles in Complete Boxes are still part of your hand.

Reading the counters

If a 16-tile hand has two completed three-tile melds, those melds contain six tiles. The remaining ten tiles are counted as Tiles In Hand: 10.

Indices shows or hides the identifying numbers and letters added to tile images. Labels switches Coaching View’s box-type labels between full and shortened names.

Pathways adds information about completion opportunities to supported developing groupings. It is available in Coaching View. Chapter 7 explains how to read it.

5. Messages and Tile Highlights

Read the instruction beneath the hand heading to see what MJC expects next, such as drawing or claiming a tile, selecting a discard, or recording a replacement draw.

Coaching messages may draw attention to your Eye, a special-hand pursuit, or a deferred Kang. These messages provide context for the hand currently recorded.

When available during a discard step, Recommended Discard identifies MJC’s suggested choice. Insight explains the recommendation. Chapter 9 covers recommendations and coaching.

  • Green highlight: identifies the last-drawn tile.
  • Yellow outline: identifies a tile selected for discard.

As your hand changes, MJC may move tiles between displayed groupings. Follow the tile names, box headings, and counters to understand the new arrangement.
